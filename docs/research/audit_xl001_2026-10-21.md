# Independent audit of XL-001 — the gate pass was a timing artifact

**Date:** 2026-10-21
**Verdict:** **XL-001 is rejected.** Its status changed from `candidate` to
`rejected`. No forward observations will be collected for it.
**Scope:** independent reimplementation of the candidate and the cost model
(`scripts/audit_xl001_independent.py`), plus a price-basis study
(`scripts/audit_xl001_price_basis.py`). Both share **no code** with
`qts.research.edgelab`.

---

## 1. What was already claimed

XL-001 (`easing_conditions_6m`, hash `2c25b9fda8eebf01`) passed all five
pre-registered gate checks and was reported in round 6 as the project's first
candidate:

| Period | Alpha/yr | alpha_t | Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|
| DEV 1971-2000 | +3.3% | 2.70 | 0.62 |
| VAL 2001-2010 | +1.4% | 0.65 | 0.92 |
| TEST 2011-2019 | +4.9% | 2.94 | 0.66 |

## 2. What the audit confirmed

Everything except the one thing that mattered.

**The cost model is correct.** Recomputing per-side basis points from the published
spread, commission and slippage, with no reference to `CostModel`:

| Model | Computed @ $2000/oz | Published |
|---|---|---|
| `ecn_assumed` | 0.4000 bps | 0.40 |
| `ecn_realistic` | 0.9250 bps | 0.925 |
| `stressed x2` | 0.8000 bps | 0.80 |
| `standard_account` | 0.5500 bps | 0.55 |

The factor-of-ten documentation error found in round 6 is genuinely fixed. A
useful detail the scorecard never printed: **cost is charged at each bar's own
price, not at the $2000 reference**, so the realistic model costs 4.62 bps/side at
gold's 1971-2000 average price and 0.56 bps/side at 2025 prices. That is correct
behaviour — a fixed dollar spread is a larger fraction of a cheaper metal — but it
means every published "0.925 bps/side" figure understates what the early sample
actually paid.

**The signal does not look ahead in its own right.** Recomputing it on history
truncated at 1995-06-15 reproduces the full-history values exactly on all 6,059
overlapping dates.

**The arithmetic reproduces.** Independent OLS on a hand-rolled backtest gives
DEV alpha_t 2.60 / TEST 2.94 against published 2.70 / 2.94; DSR at 11 trials 0.955
against 0.962. These are small differences traceable to edge effects in the
first-bar convention, not to a defect.

## 3. What the audit found

The gold series is the **LBMA AM fix, struck at 10:30 London**. The source dataset
states this explicitly (`AU.csv`: *"Daily price at 10:30 (London time)"*), and it
is the FRED `GOLDAMGBD228NLBM` AM fix.

DXY and the 10-year yield are **close-of-day** series, settling around 22:00 London.

So the harness was asking the question *"given what I know at 22:00 tonight, what
should I have bought at 10:30 this morning?"* — and then crediting the position
with the move from that morning's fix to the next one. Roughly eleven hours of
that return predate the information.

This is not a small effect. The AM fix and a 24-hour tradable close share only
**0.27 daily-return correlation** with each other (2004-2024 overlap, n=5,016):
they are different snapshots of the same metal, and gold moves a lot between them.

## 4. The test that settles it

Same rule, same dates, same costs, two price series, and the decision lagged to a
lag the data actually permits.

| Price basis | Lag | Alpha/yr | alpha_t |
|---|---|---|---|
| LBMA AM fix | 0 (as scored) | +3.93% | **2.66** |
| LBMA AM fix | 1 (implementable) | +1.02% | 0.70 |
| M15 tradable close | 0 | +1.82% | 1.23 |
| **M15 tradable close** | **1** | **+1.55%** | **1.04** |

On genuinely tradable prices the effect is **+1.6%/yr at t 1.04** — not significant,
and a third of the reported size. The gate threshold is 2.5.

Decade by decade on the implementable version, the pattern is worse than "weaker":

| Period | Lag 0 alpha_t | Lag 1 alpha_t |
|---|---|---|
| 1971-1980 | 1.64 | 2.17 |
| 1981-1990 | 2.54 | 2.51 |
| 1991-2000 | −0.06 | −0.69 |
| 2001-2010 | 0.65 | **−0.50** |
| 2011-2019 | 2.86 | 1.73 |
| 2020-2024 | 1.30 | **−0.37** |

The two most recent windows both flip negative once the trade is executable. Full
sample 1971-2024: +3.34% (t 3.88) as scored becomes +2.27% (t 2.60) implementable —
and that surviving t rests entirely on the 1970s and 1980s.

Independently, on M15 24-hour closes where the drivers *do* precede the close
(DXY settles 22:00 UTC, the bar closes 24:00 UTC, so lag 0 is legitimate here):

| Period | Alpha/yr | alpha_t |
|---|---|---|
| 2005-2010 | +2.33% | 0.86 |
| 2011-2019 | +2.45% | 1.82 |
| 2020-2024 | +0.88% | 0.38 |
| **Full 2005-2025** | **+1.57%** | **1.34** |

## 5. Why the earlier rounds did not catch this

Every macro round (3, 5, 6, 8) used the fix for gold and closes for drivers, so the
same bias runs through all of them. Round 8's holdout was run on the fix too, which
means its "MIXED" verdict was measuring the same contaminated construction — the
one genuinely reassuring thing about it is that even contaminated it went flat.

Round 3 had already found the sibling of this bug: `tsmom_1m`'s 51-year alpha
halved when moved from the fix to tradable closes. The lesson was recorded and then
not applied to the macro families. That is the process failure here — a known
artifact class was documented but never made mandatory.

## 6. What changed in the code

1. `Candidate` gained `status` and `rejected_reason`. XL-001 is `rejected`; its
   definition and hash are preserved so the rejection is reproducible.
2. `GET /api/research/edgelab-forward` reports `status`, `rejected_candidates` and
   `active_candidates`, and can never mark a rejected candidate `validated`.
3. New module `qts.research.edgelab.timing` makes the failure mode a first-class,
   tested concept: every price source and driver declares the UTC hour at which it
   is struck, `required_lag()` derives the minimum executable delay, and
   `verdict_at_lag()` fails a result that only works at an impossible lag.
4. `scripts/audit_xl001_independent.py` exits non-zero on any number that does not
   reproduce, so it can run in CI rather than be read once.

## 7. What this means for the programme

**Zero candidates. Zero validated edges.** The honest count of configurations is now
~96 across ten rounds.

Two lessons, both now enforced in code rather than in prose:

* **A gate tests statistics, not data integrity.** XL-001 passed alpha_t, DSR, cost
  stress, sign consistency and independent TEST significance while being built on a
  misaligned price series. Every one of those checks was satisfied by a result that
  does not exist in tradable form.
* **Turnover still binds.** XL-001's cost immunity came from trading 6.7 times a
  year; its alpha came from a timing artifact. Those are independent properties and
  the first does not protect the second.

The immediate consequence for new work is a hard rule: **any strategy driven by
close-of-day macro data must be measured on a 24-hour tradable close, never on an
AM fix**, and must show its effect at `timing.required_lag(...)`.
